Perxona Connect Kit — Demos

Two demos. Both hold a conversation the avatar speaks aloud, on the same backend in server.mjs and the same <sv-presenter> component — what differs is which side of the work you are looking at. Take them in order: the chatbot you make in the first is the one the second answers through.

  • Studio Start here — the console you build it from. Browse the catalog, create and edit chatbots with knowledge files and function tools, interrupt the avatar mid-sentence, watch the SDK's events in a live timeline. A switch chooses who runs the model: a Connect-hosted chatbot or your own LLM_API_KEY — flip it mid-conversation and the same history carries over.
  • Embed — what it looks like once it is live. A product site with an avatar answering questions on it. No controls, no settings, nothing on the page about its own configuration: the avatar and the chatbot are already chosen by the time a visitor arrives. Studio first. Embed answers through a Connect chatbot, and a new account doesn't have one yet — that's the normal first step, not a problem. Open Studio, press Save on the form it opens for you, then come back here.
  • Signer ASL — the SDK used in reverse. A Deaf signer signs to the camera, the browser recognizes it on-device, and the avatar says the sentence aloud to a hearing listener. Recognition is fully offline (MediaPipe Holistic + DTW) and no video ever leaves the machine; Connect does the half it is good at — speaking, with a face. Nothing beyond present() is billed. Record signs first. The vocabulary is recorded by the user, so open the demo, record a few signs 3–5 times each under Record signs, then switch to Recognize.
